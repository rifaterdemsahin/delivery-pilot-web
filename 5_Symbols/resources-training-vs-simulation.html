🎓 Classical Training vs
Simulation Workshops

In classical training, a teacher decides what everyone learns. In simulation workshops, each person follows their own curiosity — and learns far more deeply as a result.

📖

Classical Training

One topic. One presenter. Everyone receives the same content at the same pace — whether it's relevant to them or not.

VS
🧪

Simulation Workshops

Open environment. Each person explores what interests them, builds what matters to them, and learns by doing.

A Deeper Look

Understanding what each model is optimised for — and why that matters for AI upskilling.

📖 Classical Training

  • 🎤 A single expert stands at the front and delivers a prepared presentation. The agenda is set before the session begins.
  • 👥 Every participant receives identical content. A junior developer and a senior architect sit through the same slides.
  • 🕐 The clock drives progress. Topics are covered because they are scheduled, not because participants are ready.
  • 📝 Learners are consumers of information. They are evaluated on whether they can recall what was presented.
  • ⚠️ Irrelevant content must still be endured. Participants disengage when the material doesn't match their context.

🧪 Simulation Workshops

  • 🧭 The environment is open and rich. Participants enter with a goal — and the path they take is their own.
  • 🔍 Each person pursues what is most relevant and interesting to them. A developer digs into code; a PM explores decision flows.
  • 🚀 Progress is driven by curiosity and momentum. People naturally move faster on topics they care about.
  • 🛠️ Learners are builders and problem-solvers. They are evaluated on what they create and how they think.
  • 🤝 Peer knowledge sharing emerges naturally. Participants teach each other based on what they discover.

💡 Why Friction Is a Feature, Not a Bug

In classical training, confusion is hidden — participants smile and nod to avoid slowing the group down. In simulation workshops, confusion is the learning trigger. When a participant hits a wall, they are motivated to find an answer because it matters to what they are building. That motivated search produces retention that no slide deck can replicate.

When to Use Each Approach

Classical training and simulation workshops are not enemies — they serve different purposes. The key is knowing which one to reach for.

📖 Use Classical Training When...

  • Everyone must learn the exact same procedure (compliance, safety, legal)
  • The content is stable and unlikely to change quickly
  • A single authoritative answer exists and must be communicated uniformly
  • The audience has no existing context and needs structured foundations first
  • Regulatory certification requires documented delivery to all participants

🧪 Use Simulation Workshops When...

  • The domain is complex, evolving, or highly contextual — like AI
  • Different team members need to develop different skills from a shared experience
  • You want people to actually use the knowledge in their daily work afterward
  • Curiosity, creativity, and problem-solving are the desired outcomes
  • You want a team to discover their own questions, not just answer yours

🧠 The AI Upskilling Case

AI skills cannot be taught in a classical sense. The field moves too fast, the applications are too varied, and the "right answer" depends entirely on each team's context. A developer building a RAG pipeline needs different depth than a product manager evaluating LLM outputs. Simulation workshops honour that reality. Everyone starts from the same shared environment, then diverges according to what they actually need — producing a team where each member has deep, relevant, applicable knowledge rather than a room full of people who sat through the same deck.
